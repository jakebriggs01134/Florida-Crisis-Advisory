What Is Red Tide?

Red tide is a harmful algal bloom that can affect marine life, water quality, and human health. In Florida, blooms of Karenia brevis can produce toxins that harm fish and irritate the eyes, nose, and throat.

What You Should Do

  • Avoid swimming where dead fish or signs of a bloom are present.
  • Stay away from affected beaches if you experience coughing, throat irritation, or breathing discomfort.
  • Keep pets away from affected water and dead marine animals.
  • Follow local shellfish harvesting and seafood advisories.
  • People with asthma or other respiratory conditions should take extra precautions near affected beaches.

Understand the Risk

  • Storm surge: Ocean water can move inland and flood coastal communities.
  • Strong winds: Flying debris and falling trees can cause serious injuries.
  • Heavy rainfall: Flooding can occur far from the coast.
  • Tornadoes: Hurricanes can produce tornadoes, including away from the storm's center.

Turn Around, Don't Drown

Types of Flooding

Flash Flooding

Rapid flooding can develop with little warning after intense rainfall.

Coastal Flooding

Storm surge, high tides, and coastal weather can push water into low-lying areas.

River and Inland Flooding

Rivers, canals, and drainage systems can overflow after prolonged or heavy rainfall.
